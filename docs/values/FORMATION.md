## 1. What prompted it, and what the examination found

The author's document asks how Ipsissima "does and should interact with actually reading a
paper. What is gained by using Ipsissima, and what if anything is lost?" It distinguishes
three workflows — the map made *after* reading, as an adjunct; the text *studied in*
Ipsissima, back and forth between source and map; the map used *instead of* reading — and
asks of each what it displaces and whether the exchange is a net gain. That is a question
the fidelity apparatus cannot answer. The keystone asks, of everything, *how far does this
stand from the text?* — a property of the map. The document asks *where does the reader end
up?* — a property of the reader's formation. A perfectly faithful map, every span verified,
every departure warranted, can still leave its reader worse formed than the unassisted
reading it displaced.

The examination found the structure the Second Thoughts episode found: the worry contradicts
no stated principle — it exposes a premise no principle states and nearly every surface
assumes. Second Thoughts removed *a map is a map of a text*. This episode's hidden premise
is:

> **Intelligibility delivered is understanding formed.**

P2 measures the map — intelligible, checkable at every step. The author's document observes
that the measurement has two sides that can come apart, because some of the difficulty the
map removes was doing formative work. The framework predicted a gap of roughly this shape —
`THEORY.md` §6 item 4, values with no principle yet — and this is the largest instance so
far found.

Four findings give the episode its vocabulary:

**Accidental and intrinsic difficulty.** The document locates much of the cognitive load of
argumentative reading in accident: arguments "unclearly or ambiguously stated, or only
hinted at, or … presented by the author in a way that makes the ideas unnecessarily
difficult to engage with." That difficulty is no part of what a reader should be formed by,
and machining it away is pure service. But *extracting determinate structure from
underdetermining prose* is not accident — in philosophy it is close to being the discipline
itself — and the learning sciences say the same from the other side: difficulty intrinsic to
the target skill is where the learning happens (the generation and testing effects; Bjork's
"desirable difficulties"), while difficulty extraneous to it is cost without return. The cut
yields the episode's lodestar: **offload the accidental difficulty; preserve — even stage —
the intrinsic difficulty.** Proposed as B8.

**Where the atrophy worry attaches.** Given the cut, the document's atrophy question stops
attaching to Ipsissima simpliciter and attaches to one axis: *who did the reconstruction,
and what does the reader do with it*. The tool already spans that axis — consuming a
machine-made map (maximum offload, and the only position where atrophy has real purchase);
studying text-with-map; building the map from the text, where the guided from-a-text mode
is, read through this lens, a close-reading scaffold: syntax and bookkeeping machined away,
which-sentence and what-relation left entirely with the student. The design consequence is a
*gradient*, not a subsystem: make movement from consumption toward construction easy and
inviting (§6, items 3–4).

**The propaedeutic clause.** The document adapts the Stanford Encyclopedia's editorial
standard to the single-source case: Ipsissima should introduce someone "who may have no
special knowledge of the topic" to the source's issues, claims and arguments, "so as to
bring their readers to a state where they can read [the source text(s)] with insight and
understanding." That is a telos: the map as a way *into* the text, not past it. Tested
against the three workflows it cannot stand absolute — the adjunct workflow is a way back
and deeper, not in; and some terminal uses are legitimate (the document's own Dewey point;
the paper that only deserved its abstract). The defensible form is displacement-relative,
and is proposed as B9.

**Determinacy, and the Kant deficiency.** A reconstruction is by nature more determinate
than its text. The apparatus marks that a claim is interpretive; nothing marks that *rivals
exist*. The document's report from the Critique of Judgement map: "it presented only one
interpretation as if this were _the_ interpretation — rather than (as we actually teach such
texts) there being an expectation that the reader will need to struggle with the different
available interpretations." The examination's finding is that the deepest answer is not
inside any one map: the commentary traditions that solved apparatus-beside-text (the glossed
page, the variorum) stayed healthy because the commentaries *disagreed*, and the classroom
design already manufactures the cure — fifty students, one text, fifty files. Plurality over
hedging: §3 T4 and §6 items 1–2 carry the proposals.

Two further observations the tensions pick up: **anchoring** — once a plausible map has been
seen, the reading the reader would have formed is hard to recover, which touches even the
adjunct workflow (T2's elicit-before-show, §6 item 4); and **portfolio displacement** — the
document's counterfactual is per-text, but a tool this cheap changes the population of
engagements, in either direction (texts once skimmed now studied; or forty maps commissioned
and nothing read), which the tool cannot control but its defaults and rhetoric nudge (T5,
§6 items 6–7).

## 3. The tensions, proposed

Named in advance, with a candidate resolution each, awaiting the author's rulings:

- **T1 — Does the propaedeutic clause hold across workflows and users?** Absolute, it fails
  the adjunct workflow and moralises at the triage reader. Candidate resolution: B9 binds
  displacement-relative and road-not-journey — the tool must keep the way from any claim to
  the words it reads open, visible and short, and must not present the map as more terminal
  than it is; but the reader's own terminal purpose is sovereign (B6; P3-reader). The tool
  protects the road, never prescribes the journey.
- **T2 — Does staged difficulty collide with novice-first intelligibility?** B1 tests every
  design against the novice; B2 gives the reader their own pace; P6 forbids unpurposed
  claims on attention. A study mode that *withholds* — folds a contention's support and asks
  the reader what they think holds it up before unfolding — deliberately makes the map
  harder. Candidate resolution: the staircase precedent transfers — **legible default,
  effortful option**: staging is offered, never imposed, on the walkthrough's pattern
  (claimed once, easily exited, always recallable), and the same rule covers
  elicit-before-show. Candidate case-law row: *novice legibility vs formative staging — the
  legible default, the effortful option kept*.
- **T3 — Unredeemed checkability.** For the reader who never opens the source, the fidelity
  borders risk functioning as reassurance for a check no human made: the spans are
  machine-verified, but the *reading* has been vouched for by no one. The keystone's own
  vocabulary names it — a representation declared checkable but never checked. The
  `formalized:` stamp is the project's existing shape for the fix: the machine checks what
  it can, a human attestation the machine refuses to fake records the rest. Candidate
  resolution: put the question, not the machinery — is a lightweight whole-map attestation
  ("a person has read this map against the text") a real debt or over-engineering? The null
  option beside it: one honest sentence in the orientation panel, and nothing else. No gate
  in either case.
- **T4 — One reading presented as the reading.** The Kant deficiency. Hedging every claim
  would bury the map in qualifications and fail P6; saying nothing presents an
  interpretation as the text's own settlement and fails A1's spirit. Candidate resolution:
  **plurality over hedging**, at three scales — the reconstructor records *interpretive
  forks* in `note:`s where a choice among live readings was made (within the language, no
  dialect; A9); the census counts declared forks, and where interpretive load is high the
  orientation panel says in one line that this is one reading among defensible readings —
  measured, not moralised; and comparison of same-text maps becomes first-class (§6 items
  1–2). The draft-mode precedent governs the first: naming underdetermination is a result,
  not a failure.
- **T5 — May the self-description claim learning benefits?** The document's judgement that
  Ipsissima is "a cognitive upgrade" over abstracts and skimming is, in E1's terms,
  [judgement] — plausible, unmeasured. Candidate resolution: the honesty-over-marketing row
  already binds — the tool claims what is measured (spans verified, structure checkable) and
  does not claim what is not (learning outcomes), until teachers' adoption generates the
  field data the class-of-fifty target was reframed to await (`EDITOR-PLAN.md` §2).

## 6. The downstream queue

The word was given for the queue as drafted (ruling 6), and the author's question about
items 1 and 2 settled their boundary before either was built:

**One file, one reading — multiplicity at three scales (settled 14 Sep).** A reconstruction
asserts exactly one reading: the front matter's reading policy declares *which* reading the
file attempts, and that machinery presumes a singular answer. Multiplicity then lives at
three scales, and the scale is the criterion:

- **The floor — every fork gets a note.** Wherever the reconstructor chose among live
  readings, the `note:` names the reading followed and the road not taken, attributed
  where scholarship divides.
- **The chip — `#crux`.** The same claim carries the tag, so the contested points are
  countable and filterable. The existing tag census counts it with **zero checker
  changes** — "the census counts declared forks" fell out of machinery already built.
- **The reported rival — in the map, at local junctures.** Where a rival reading is local
  and load-bearing, it appears the way an opponent's position already does: `#reported`
  claims, attributed to whose reading it is. The map still asserts one reading; it
  *reports* another at the point where the disagreement bites. Existing A10 machinery, no
  new mechanism.
- **The sibling file — item 2's machinery, for global rivals.** A rival that re-wires the
  argument, moves the apex, or changes the reading policy is a different reconstruction,
  and hashtag-multiplexing cannot carry it: the drawn graph would hold both wirings at
  once — a union-picture asserting a reading nobody holds, the F1 family's failure (the
  picture saying what no interpretation says); the computed apex and census would
  aggregate across readings; and neither reading would be individually checkable. Sibling
  files keep each reading a coherent, checkable assertion with its own reading policy,
  and the comparison surface computes their agreement and disagreement. So item 2's
  machinery is not a better display of what hashtags do — it is the only honest carrier
  of the global case, while hashtags (`#crux`, `#reported`) remain the right tool at the
  local scale.

The waves, in the drafted order:

1. **Interpretive forks in the extraction method — landed 14 Sep.** The conventions
   document defines `#crux` (tag table row and its own subsection, with the three-scale
   discipline); the extraction prompt instructs it at the point of writing; the method
   document grounds it in the charity analysis (§2.5, beside Stern's `unit: meaning`).
   The census change was nothing: the tag census already counts per-claim tags. (T4's
   first scale; the draft-mode precedent.)
2. **Same-text plurality in the App.** An identity for "readings of the same source" and a
   surface for comparing two maps of one text — side by side, or their disagreements
   diffed. The classroom convergence is the warrant: fifty files of one text are the
   plurality the Kant worry wants *and* the empirical dataset the document says these
   questions need, already sitting in the teacher's hands.
3. **Folding as retrieval practice.** A study option that folds a contention's support and
   asks the reader to predict before unfolding — the testing effect built from machinery
   that already exists, offered on the walkthrough's pattern. (T2's terms.)
4. **Elicit-before-show.** An optional door that asks the reader to commit their candidate
   conclusion before an existing map opens — the guided mode's step 1 turned to face a
   finished map; D7's *interrogative, not generative* extended to the reader's own reading,
   against anchoring. (T2's terms govern its offer.)
5. **The orientation panel's one-reading sentence**, census-driven, where interpretive
   load or the crux count is high. (T4's second scale.) This wave also inherits T3's
   refined ruling: the read-by-a-human line, if drawn, is a declaration and not a
   certification, worded to claim no more reassurance than checking a hundred-claim map
   against its source can actually deliver. Found while landing wave 1: **the data half
   of T3's line already exists** — the `reviewed:` key records a person's pass over the
   map, and the checker already guards it with the `formalized:` stamp's own rule (never
   set on a generated map; the reviewer sets it, removing `generated: true` in the same
   pass). What remains is the App's sentence, bounded as the ruling requires.
6. **Text-first or map-first?** Which pane a reading-genre map opens into is a values
   decision wearing a default's clothes; the question is put rather than answered, with
   B9's road-not-journey as the test.
7. **The self-description sweep.** Site and README checked against T5's rule: no unmeasured
   learning claims; the map sold as an instrument of reading, not its replacement.
8. **The persona question, joined.** The Modest Proposal sample's open `#reported`-for-
   persona question is the document's Swift point arriving in the machinery: a text whose
   value is not its argument may deserve a front-matter declaration to that effect, so the
   map can honestly say the argument is the least of what the text is. To be answered
   together with T4's conventions rather than separately.
