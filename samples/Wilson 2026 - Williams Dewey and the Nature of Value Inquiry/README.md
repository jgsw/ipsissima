*Philosophy* 101 (2026), pp. 511–538. Twenty-eight pages, 10,381 words, eight numbered sections.
Open **`wilson-williams-dewey (map).html`**.

**The third test of `extraction-prompt.md`, and the one that strains it.** The Carroll states no
conclusion and everything must be imputed; the Horton states everything; this paper does
something the prompt's list of forms has no entry for. Its author describes the form as closer to
music than to "I will assert that P": themes are introduced, modulate, interweave, and resolve as
the conclusion arrives.

## The reconstruction

**Three contentions, not one — and that is not a defect of the map.** Forcing a single apex would
mean inventing a super-claim the paper does not make, which is an imputation at the very top: the
thing the Carroll map had to do and this paper does not need. The three are connected rather than
three separate trees, and the paper states them in three different places:

1. **What value inquiry requires**, from the abstract — what is needed "is less a shift from
   scientistic to humanistic conceptions of philosophy, than for philosophers working in value
   inquiry to better align their aspirations for theory with what it can actually deliver". A
   comparative thesis whose two halves are the paper's two lines of argument.
2. **The debate is not deadlocked** (§5, p. 16): the dispute between Williams and the moral
   realists is not a standoff, because the sustained mismatch between philosophy's ambition and
   its progressiveness is itself evidence for Williams.
3. **Dewey is the nimbler thinker** (§8, p. 25) about what philosophy might become.

They are connected, and by routes the map makes visible: contentions 1 and 2 share
"Philosophy is ambitious without being progressive" as a premise, and contention 3 is reached
from §1's disciplinary result by way of §7's denial that philosophy may criticise other
disciplines while staying immune from their critique.

142 claims and 20 arguments, drawn as 161 nodes — one claim is an intermediate conclusion
and sits inside its argument rather than beside it — with 173 edges and 8 groups.
**91 quotation · 47 paraphrase · 18 compression · 5 interpretation.**
139 of 139 quotations verify exactly. 142 of 142 claims are located, 139 pinned by a quotation and
3 to a paragraph. Nothing is disconnected and nothing is inert: 98 claims support a contention,
41 engage one by objecting, and none fails to reach one by some route.

The five interpretations each carry a warrant — three coherence, two enthymeme — and the
interpretive-load report reads zero at all three contentions, meaning some route to each runs on
reported material alone. `#reported` does the heaviest tagging work in the corpus here (59 of 142
claims), because a claim in this paper may be Williams's, Dewey's, a critic's, or the paper's own,
and the map has no other way to say which.

**The map opens where the paper's argument opens, not where its own claims start.** An earlier
version began on p. 3 with the disciplinary result, and the by-position view is what showed the
problem: a map that starts where the author's own claims start leaves out what those claims are
*about*. The rebuild keeps the repair. Coverage now begins at the abstract and §1 — Williams's
humanistic conception, his two objections to scientism, and the limit he sets on his own target.

## What the map shows better than a reading

**The interweaving becomes measurable.** In a 161-node map only three claims carry three outgoing
edges apiece, and seven more nodes carry two — but which three is the point:

- **"No overall answer to where philosophy fits"** (§1, p. 3) feeds the abstract's thesis, §6's
  verdict that the blunt categorical question about thought experiments is unhelpful, and §7's
  denial of philosophy's asymmetric right of critique.
- **"Ethical freedom"** (§4, p. 12) is a premise in three separate arguments: Williams's
  commitments hanging together, fecundity rather than homogeneity as the sign of a good case in
  §6, and what ethical freedom implies for method.
- **"Limits on convergence in sense-making"** (§4, p. 10) is a premise in two arguments and stands
  contradictory to the realists' reply that ethical theory could yet become vindicatory.

In the prose these are themes returned to; in the map they are single nodes feeding three places
at once, which is the one thing a map does better than reading.

**The map's eight groups are not the paper's eight sections.** Only one group is a single section
("Cases, fiction and the arts", all of §6). "The meta-problem, and the case for modesty" gathers
§5's argument for modesty together with §1's verdict that the evidence favours Williams and §7's
denial of an asymmetric right of critique, and then concludes in a §8 sentence. "What ethical
freedom would mean for method" is a single §4 argument that pays out nowhere until §8. Where the
groups cut across the numbering is where the themes cross.

**And the long-range structure shows up in the numbers.** Thirteen claims are stated twenty or
more claims before anything draws on them. Six of the eight longest-carried are the apparatus of
the middle-range proposal — ambition as a vector of generality and precision, progressiveness as
a vindicatory history, the mismatch between them, testability, Merton's precedent in sociology,
and §4's warning that general ethical theory may make judgement worse. They are stated across
§§4–5 and none is drawn on until claim 138, because the sentence they support is one the paper
does not write until §8, p. 25 — the widest gap of all, 75 claims, belongs to §4's warning. One
more is carried right across the paper: "Williams's view better fits the evidence", stated at
claim 16 in §1, p. 4 and first drawn on at claim 80, when §5's mismatch argument reaches it. Dewey enters at §3, p. 8, quoted on problematisation, seventeen pages before
he is named as the destination. The CARRIED LONGEST report finds exactly this, and on a linear
argument it would be a defect.

## What the map shows worse

**An argument map is atemporal by construction.** The paper is meant to be experienced in time,
and the map flattens that by design. For this paper the **Order view is not a supplement to the
argument view — it is the half that carries the form.**

**And the apex list no longer shows one of the paper's own conclusions.** §8 sets out what the
paper found, and the first thing it reports is that there are reasons to doubt whether philosophy
can fruitfully be assigned either to the humanities or to the sciences. In the map that claim is
not an apex, because the paper goes on to *use* it twice more — in §6 to dismiss the categorical
question about thought experiments, and in §7 to deny philosophy an asymmetric right of critique.
Having outgoing edges, it is drawn as an intermediate conclusion, and drawn that way correctly:
those are real inferential uses, and suppressing them to promote the claim to an apex would lose
two arguments the paper actually makes. But the apex list then reports three contentions where
the paper's own conclusion section announces this one first. The house rule "read the apex list
before believing any map" assumes a claim is either a conclusion or a premise. In a paper whose
themes return, a claim can be both, and the heuristic has no way to say so.

## One claim, and where the join gets drawn

The paper's §1 runs:

> I argue that it is a mistake to think that there is an overall answer to the question whether
> philosophy belongs with the humanities or the sciences. Rather, the philosophical methods that
> it is most fruitful to adopt depend on the research question to be addressed (a point that
> Williams's own philosophical practice showcases, even if his metaphilosophy makes this less
> clear).

An earlier map made this a single claim, running the two sentences together and dropping the
`Rather,`. That presents as one continuous statement something the author wrote as two thoughts —
Stern's misreporting structure, in miniature, and the sort of thing a marker would pick a student
up on.

The rebuilt map draws them as two claims and an edge. "No overall answer to where philosophy
fits" is the first sentence; "Method should follow the research problem" is the second, standing
as the conclusion of §3's argument from problematisation and supporting the first. The note on
that conclusion says why it is drawn rather than run together. Nothing is elided, and the
relation the `Rather,` marks is a relation in the map instead of a splice inside a claim.

`check_argdown.py` reports **unmarked splices**: claims made mostly of the source's words that
join passages far apart without saying so. This file has none. Five claims do elide, each keeping
a `source:` span the checker verifies against the text, and the two that cut something
load-bearing — the parenthesis in the paper's account of what makes an activity a science, and the
two halves of Parfit's footnote — carry a note naming what was cut.

## Conversion

`pdf_to_source.py` refuses this paper, correctly: it is set with **different margins on recto and
verso** (x0=42.8 and 65.8), each with its own paragraph indent, so the global band detector sees
four left edges where there are two. The blocks reflow in `ingest.py` handles that without caring
— but leaves the running heads inline and finds no headings, and **the headings cannot be found
by typography at all**: they are set in the same 11pt as the body, and only the author's numbering
marks them. Hence this folder's own converter: the shared reflow, plus heading promotion,
furniture removal, and de-hyphenation.

```bash
python3 convert_source.py
python3 ../../ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp/check_argdown.py \
    wilson-williams-dewey.argdown --source-root . --no-fix
node "../../app/build_argdown_viewer.mjs" \
    wilson-williams-dewey.argdown --source-root .
```

`--no-fix` is needed to read the census without changing the file: the reconstruction declares
`generated: true`, and for such a file the checker corrects `quotation`/`paraphrase` markers
against the source by default.
