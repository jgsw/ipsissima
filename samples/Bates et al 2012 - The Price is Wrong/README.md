## Why this one is here

It is the first sample that is a **policy text** rather than philosophy or law, and the first with
a **mechanism**: the chain of steps the text says runs from what it recommends to what that
recommendation is for, drawn in the Mechanism arrangement beside the argument.

The two are different things, and this text shows why both are needed. The argument is a
conductive policy case: ten randomized evaluations answer each rationale for charging (revenue,
use, targeting, provider incentives) and each worry about giving products away (resentment,
resale), and the balance yields the verdict that small fees "may be the worst of both worlds".
The mechanism is what the text says *happens* — fees cut take-up, take-up is what use and health
run through — and most of its central results are findings of **no effect**: paying does not
make people use a product more, fees do not target the needy. A causal diagram that could only
draw arrows would have nothing to say about the text's main findings. Here each null is drawn
against the rival view's arrow it answers, and the one **screening effect** the text found is
drawn as a selection link — who ends up owning chlorine — not as an effect of the price.

## The mechanism

Five kinds of actor at two levels (programmes and providers; households and children), 21 states,
and 27 distinct steps asserted by 47 claims: 14 backed by a study, 2 argued, 10 asserted only, and
1 imputed where the text's own inference needs it (a higher cost per person means lower
cost-effectiveness). Six findings of no effect, four of them set against a rival view's step; one
selection link. The chain stops at six states the text leads into and no further — among them
providers' effort and health workers' leakage — and those gaps are the text's, reported rather
than closed. The notes on each state say which of the text's words were read as one state and
which were kept apart; those are the decisions two readers are likeliest to make differently.

The checker's coverage report finds 37 of the text's 73 sentences of causal language quoted by a
step, and 20 more in a paragraph with one.

## How it was made

- **The text**: `make_source.py` converts the reprint's PDF with Ipsissima's own route and then
  makes the repairs a two-column layout needs — headings, Table 1, the boxes moved out of the
  sentences they split, chart axes and page furniture dropped — and checks that no word of the
  authors' was changed, lost or added. The PDF is not here; the script takes its path.
- **The argument map** was generated by a model driving Ipsissima-MCP (`generated: true`), first
  against the 2011 bulletin and then carried over to the reprint, where every quotation was
  checked again and every pinpoint moved to the reprint's printed pages.
- **The mechanism** was added afterwards by the separate mechanism pass
  (`ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp/docs/mechanism-pass.md`), which re-reads the source and may
  add to the map but not change its argument. No person has yet reviewed either (no `reviewed:`
  stamp).

To rebuild the source:

```bash
python3 make_source.py path/to/factsreports-1554.pdf
```
