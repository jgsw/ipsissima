# The Coleman boat

Read this when the text sets out, or argues by, the macro-micro-macro diagram. It is drawn in the
mechanism notation (`mechanism-notation`).

- **A Coleman boat** — the macro-micro-macro diagram, and any text that argues by it — is drawn as a
  boat where it is marked as one. `levels: [macro, micro]`, the larger first, with `ordering: scale`
  unless the text says its levels are parts within wholes (Coleman's macro is a matter of scale:
  Ylikoski 2016). The corners are states: A and D at the upper level, B and C at the lower, in the
  text's words; the letters go in a label only where the text uses them. Arrows 1 to 3 are steps,
  each with the `mark:` the text gives it and the `name:` it gives the kind (situational,
  action-formation, transformational). Arrow 4, A to D, as the text has it: no step where the text's
  boat has none; `association: true` where it reports only that the two go together; where it says
  A brought D about through B and C, a step with `via: [B, C]` and the `mark:` the text gives it
  ("4", or Ylikoski's "?"). A numbered summary is drawn; an unnumbered one is listed in the panel.
  The feedback from what agents do to their situation (Ylikoski's fifth arrow; arrow 3 in
  Martínez-Peña and Ylikoski 2024) is a step from C back to B. Where the text breaks B into parts
  (action situation, mental states, capacities), declare B and give each part `part_of: B`. Mark
  arrow 1 as the text states it; where the text treats A as partly made up of B, that part is
  `constitutes:`, not a step. Boats chained in time are chains, the D of one the A of the next
  (`roles:`).
