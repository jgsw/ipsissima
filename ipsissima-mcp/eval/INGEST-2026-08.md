# Five documents the pipeline had not seen — measured 27 Aug 2026

Four ingest fixtures added, one document unreachable. The point of the exercise was coverage of
formats rather than of arguments, so nothing here was reconstructed and nothing here needs to be.

What it found is that **the two PDF routes fail on opposite documents**, and that neither is safe
to make the default. `pdf_to_source.py` discards 41% of the Dewey and loses all 71 of the
Miller's paragraph numbers; `ingest.py` gets both of those right and then leaves 94 Dewey words
broken in half, because it has no de-hyphenation step at all. On the Ramsey the ranking inverts
again.

## What was added

| file | format | licence, and how it was verified | size |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dewey-1896-reflex-arc.pdf` | 1896 scan, 14 pp | Internet Archive item marked `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`; published 1896 | 848 KB |
| `ramsey-1928-mathematical-theory-of-saving.pdf` | 1928 microfilm, 17 pp | published 1928, author d. 1930 — out of copyright in the UK (life+70, expired 2000) and the US (pre-1929) | 2.7 MB |
| `miller-2019-uksc-41.pdf` | born-digital, 25 pp | the Court's own terms page: Crown copyright material from that site is re-usable **under the Open Government Licence**, reproduced accurately | 296 KB |
| `robeyns-2017-wellbeing-freedom-social-justice.pdf` | born-digital book, 268 pp | **CC-BY 4.0**, agreeing in Crossref, DOAB and Thoth's own record | 1.87 MB |

Both scans are page ranges lifted out of the bound volume or issue with `insert_pdf`, which
copies the text layer byte-for-byte. Neither was recompressed: the page image is what a human
reads a repair off, and degrading it would cost the fixture its purpose.

## What was not added, and why

**Etiévant, Gail & Follmann (2026), doi:10.1515/jci-2024-0074.** CC-BY 4.0 — confirmed in
Crossref and DOAJ, so it is freely redistributable and the licence is not the obstacle. Every De
Gruyter host, current and legacy, answers `HTTP 202` with a zero-byte body and
`x-amzn-waf-action: challenge`. It is not in Europe PMC, PMC or arXiv; DOAJ's only full-text link
is the DOI, which resolves back to the challenge. Not fetched, and not worked around.

**Miller as Akoma Ntoso XML**, from the National Archives. `robots.txt` at
`caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk` disallows `ClaudeBot`, `anthropic-ai` and `Claude-Web` by name.
That is an explicit request, not an incidental block, so the XML was not fetched. The judgment
itself came from the Supreme Court's own site instead, which permits it.

Two things follow that are worth carrying. First, **the structured-XML route remains untested** —
the fixture set still has no JATS, BITS or Akoma Ntoso document in it, and that was the one
genuinely new *format* on the list. Second, **the licence is probably not the OGL**: the
National Archives' own API catalogue points at the *Open Justice Licence v2*, which is a
different instrument from the OGL under which the UKSC publishes. If the XML is ever added by
hand, that difference wants checking first.

**Robeyns as XML.** Thoth's record lists XML among the published formats, so it exists; it is
behind the same AWS WAF challenge on `books.openbookpublishers.com`. The publisher's PDF, from
OAPEN, is what was used.

None of the three went into `fixtures/private-corpus.json`. Every entry in that file answers "why
private", and all three of these are openly licensed — the true answer is "unreachable from a
script", which is a different fact and belongs where somebody adding a fixture will read it.
`fixtures/ingest/README.md` carries it.

**A note for whoever writes the next downloader.** A WAF challenge presents as `HTTP 202` with
`Content-Length: 0`. `curl -o` writes an empty file and exits 0, and so would anything checking
only the status code. Both blocked publishers here failed in exactly that shape.

### Would a quotation verify?

The only one of the four with independent ground truth is the Dewey, which has a scholarly
transcription at *Classics in the History of Psychology* (C. D. Green, York University). Split
into 169 sentences of 12-45 words, normalised for case, punctuation and quote marks, and looked
for verbatim in each conversion:

| | sentences reproduced word-for-word |
|---|---|
| `pdf_to_source.py` | **2 / 169 — 1%** |
| `ingest.py` | **75 / 169 — 44%** |
| `ingest.py`, with the blunt ASCII de-hyphenation rule applied afterwards | **114 / 169 — 67%** |

That third row is the finding. It is the same file, with one regex applied.

## 1. `ingest.py` does not de-hyphenate, and it is the largest single loss

There is no hyphen handling anywhere in `ingest.py` — the word does not occur in the file. So
every word the typesetter broke across a printed line stays broken: 94 line-ending hyphens on the
Dewey, plus 17 more welded mid-line as `preform- ulated` and `organ- ism`.

Applying `pdf_to_source.dehyphenate()`'s ASCII branch to the finished file takes verification
from 44% to 67%. The rule is right 79 times out of 85 distinct joins on this document. The six it
gets wrong are worth naming, because they are the cost side of the trade
`trust_soft_hyphens` was written to reason about: `co-ordination` → `coordination`,
`far-reaching` → `forreaching`, `self-created` → `selfcreated`, `sensori-motor` →
`sensorimotor`, and two OCR artefacts.

**Reproducer**

```bash
.venv/bin/python ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp/ingest.py \
    fixtures/ingest/dewey-1896-reflex-arc.pdf --out /tmp/dewey
grep -cE '[A-Za-z]-$' /tmp/dewey/source/dewey-1896-reflex-arc.md      # 94
```

This is not a recommendation to bolt the blunt rule on. The Miller shows why (§4). It is a
recommendation that the decision be made somewhere rather than nowhere: at present the two routes
in this repository give opposite answers to "is a line-end hyphen part of the word", and one of
them gives no answer at all.

### And `trust_soft_hyphens` itself works

Asked for directly: all four documents have **zero** soft hyphens, against 111, 19, 3 and 0 ASCII
line-end breaks, so `trust_soft_hyphens()` returns `False` on each and the blunt branch is taken.
That is the correct branch for the two old scans, whose text layers predate the convention, and
it is what the function's docstring predicts. The Gutenberg furniture rule is also intact: the
Russell EPUB still reports `2 skipped as furniture`, and `split_manuscript --level 2` still
recovers all fifteen chapters after warning at level 1.

## 2. `sheet_lines()` sorts by `y0` alone, and the Dewey has no lines to sort

**This is the one to fix first.** The Dewey's OCR layer does not store printed lines; it stores
word-groups, each placed at its own baseline following the skew of the scanned page. One printed
line arrives as five fragments spanning 5.2 points of `y0`:

```
  y0= 233.348  x0= 165.28 | in psychology should come at
  y0= 236.456  x0=  35.53 | trolling working hypothesis
  y0= 238.506  x0= 303.52 | just
```

`sheet_lines()` ends with `return sorted(out, key=lambda l: l["y0"])` and no secondary key, so it
reads those in the order shown — right-hand fragment first. Dewey's opening sentence comes out as

> That the greater demand for a unifying principle and conin psychology should come at trolling
> working hypothesis just the time when generalizations and are most classifications all
> questioned and questionable is natural enough.

against a true reading of "…and controlling working hypothesis in psychology should come at just
the time when all generalizations and classifications are most questioned and questionable…".
`con-` is then de-hyphenated onto whatever followed the scramble, producing `conin`.

Rounding `y0` does not fix it — the spread is larger than any sane rounding unit. The fix is to
cluster fragments into printed lines by overlapping `y` extents and sort within a cluster by
`x0`, which is more than a one-line change and is why it is reported rather than done.

pymupdf4llm does this correctly, which is why `ingest.py` scores 44% and `pdf_to_source.py`
scores 1%.

**Reproducer**

```bash
.venv/bin/python ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp/pdf_to_source.py \
    fixtures/ingest/dewey-1896-reflex-arc.pdf /tmp/dewey.md
grep -c conin /tmp/dewey.md     # 1
```

## Findings 1 and 2 are fixed, and fixing 1 exposed what it costs

**`sheet_lines()`** — a `reading_order()` now bands lines whose baselines fall within 0.6 of the
median glyph size and reads each band left to right. On the Dewey that is 4.5pt against a 3.5pt
fragment spread and a 9.6pt line advance, so the fragments band and the next printed line does
not. Measured on page 3:

```
before:  is looking, and not a | act of seeing; | with the | sensation of | it
after :  with the | act of seeing; | it | is looking, and not a | sensation of
```

Per column, deliberately: two columns share their `y` values, so banding a whole sheet would read
across the gutter. A clean digital PDF has no jitter and bands one line each, which is why nothing
moves on the modern papers.

**`ingest.plain_text()`** now de-hyphenates. The first attempt repaired **1 of 94** breaks, because
`pdf_to_source.py` de-hyphenates per block and is right to — there `to_blocks` has assembled real
paragraphs, so a break always falls inside one. Here a block is whatever pymupdf returned, which
on a scan is often a single printed line, so the breaks fall *between* blocks. Repairing across
the join takes Dewey and Miller both to **zero** breaks remaining, with page markers intact — and
the markers protect the page boundaries for free, since `<!-- p.4 begins here -->` does not begin
with a word character, so a word broken across a page stays broken rather than eating the marker.

### What the blunt rule costs, measured rather than asserted

`trust_soft_hyphens` documents the hazard: with no soft hyphens in a document the ASCII rule
applies, and it welds real compounds broken at a line end. Robeyns is the case to measure it on —
95,478 words, **zero** soft hyphens, so the blunt branch runs over the whole book:

| welded | correct form | occurrences of each |
|---|---|---|
| `wellknown` | well-known | 1 vs 2 |
| `nonideal` | non-ideal | 1 vs 5 |
| `decisionmaking` | decision-making | 1 vs 7 |

**Three welds in 95,478 words**, against hundreds of correct joins. (`wellbeing` at 390 and
`interpersonal` at 25 are the book's own spellings, not damage — its title carries no hyphen.)

**The refinement was made, and it removes all three.** `keep_hyphen()` collects every compound the
document spells with a hyphen *inside* a line, where no line break can be responsible, and
`dehyphenate` keeps the hyphen when the same pair meets at a break. No dictionary, no dependency:
a book that breaks "well-known" across a line writes it "well-known" everywhere else, so the
evidence is already in the text.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `wellknown` / `well-known` | 1 / 2 | **0 / 3** |
| `decisionmaking` / `decision-making` | 1 / 7 | **0 / 8** |
| `nonideal` / `non-ideal` | 1 / 5 | 1 / 5 |

The surviving `nonideal` is not a weld. It is inside a bibliography entry — Adam Swift's
*'The Value of Philosophy in Nonideal Circumstances'* — and that is the author's own spelling. So
the count is **three welds to none**.

It is conservative by construction: a pair seen hyphenated mid-line is kept, a pair never seen
that way is joined as before. It can therefore only ever prevent a weld and never cause a split,
and where a document is inconsistent with itself the hyphen wins, which is the recoverable
error.

## `BACK_MATTER` is fixed, and it was two faults at once

`references?` matched the bare singular, and on a scan whose OCR splits prose into fragments
"with reference to" leaves a line that *is* the word — twice in the Dewey. The loop that used it
then had neither the "last third" bound its own comment claimed nor a `break`, so it kept going
past a match and the **earliest** one anywhere in the paper won.

Neither fix alone was enough: of the Dewey's two false matches, one is at 63% and one at 68%, so
the bound would still have cut at the second. Plural-only kills both, the bound and the `break`
make the loop do what it always said it did, and the article now runs **0..1635 — 100% kept**,
ending on Dewey's own last sentence: *"judgment must be deferred to a more favorable
opportunity."*

## 3. `BACK_MATTER` matches the word "reference", and the loop keeps the earliest match

On the Dewey, `pdf_to_source.py` reports `back matter (detected) x596` and writes 3,384 words of
a 5,742-word article. It cut at sheet 8 — printed p.365, five pages before the end — on this:

```
   1027 x0=  75.5 | 'with'
   1028 x0= 101.7 | 'reference'      <-- BACK_MATTER matches
   1029 x0= 208.6 | 'or'
```

Dewey's own sentence, "…distinctions of the part played, with reference to reaching or
maintaining an end." The fragmented layer puts `reference` alone on a line, and two things then
compound:

- **`references?` accepts the singular.** No bibliography is ever headed "reference", and
  "reference" is an ordinary English word. The plural-optional `?` buys nothing and costs this.
- **The loop has no bound and no `break`.** `article_span()`'s comment says "the first line on
  the last third of the paper", but the loop runs `for i in range(len(rows)-1, -1, -1)` over
  *every* row and reassigns `last = i` on each match, so the **earliest** match in the document
  wins, not the last-third one. Two rows matched here, on sheets 8 and 9; the one on sheet 8 took
  37% of the article with it.

Either half alone would have been survivable. Together, one stray word discards 2,358 words, and
the report says `x596` rather than "the article now ends on p.365".

**Reproducer** — the run above; the output's last sentence is on printed p.365 and the paper
runs to p.370.

## 4. Miller: 71 numbered paragraphs in, none out — fixed, and it was four faults

A judgment is cited **by its paragraph number** — *Miller (No 2)* at [50] — so losing the numbers
loses the only address a claim in such a document has. All 71 were lost, in four different ways,
and each had to be found separately because each hid the next.

| | what it was | cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a lone number matches no indent band, so it fell through to "merge into the previous block" and was swallowed by the paragraph **above** the one it numbers | 69 |
| 2 | a number opening a page is that page's first line and has no letters in it, so the **running-head** test dropped it | 8 |
| 3 | a number low on a sheet normalises to `#` exactly as the printed page number does, so the **footer** test dropped it | 1 |
| 4 | `64. Article 9 provides:` has the shape of `2. Hume and abstraction`, so a short numbered paragraph was promoted to a **section heading** | 1 |

Two distinctions do all the work, and both were already visible in the geometry:

**A paragraph number has its paragraph beside it; a page number is alone.** On Miller the numbers
sit at x=72 with the first line of prose at x=108 on the same line, while the printed page number
is centred at x=295 with nothing beside it. `is_furniture` could not see this because it was
handed only `(text, y0, height)`; it now takes `alone` as well, and the caller — which has the
whole page — works it out.

**A heading arrives as one row; a numbered paragraph arrives as two.** That is known in
`to_blocks` and nowhere else, so the block records it and the emitter reads it, rather than trying
to tell them apart by their text, which is impossible.

Result: **71 of 71, contiguous 1..71**, no stray numbers left in the prose, and 22 page numbers
still dropped as furniture. Checked for regressions across every PDF fixture — Dewey, Ramsey,
Robeyns and Etiévant all still drop their running heads and page numbers, and none gained a stray
number.

## 5. Ramsey: the mathematics is destroyed upstream, and no converter can help

The interesting half of this paper does not survive its own scan. The Keynes-Ramsey rule's
derivation is in the text layer as

```
v(a) = ag (*) >>
Ww
er
ME
Sapien a
```

and, further down, `= oe »f v(a)g. (Using (2), (3) and (1).)`. The prose around it converts well —
5,926 words, 104 positions, all seventeen printed page numbers 543-559 recovered correctly, zero
broken words, which is `pdf_to_source.py`'s best showing of the four. The equations are gone
before any tool is chosen, and re-OCR would be attacking the same glyphs.

This is worth stating plainly because it bounds what routing can buy: **on a document like this
the converter question is settled and the answer is "no converter".** What a reconstruction can
cite from the Ramsey is its prose, and any claim resting on a displayed equation has to be
checked against the page image.

To the detector's credit, it found them. `flag_stretched()` reports 17 suspicious lines on
pp.546-556 — `oe`, `du`, `PSR FM`, `ae ie`, `iin}. ee. oe Rk <i` — which is precisely the
displayed mathematics, located by geometry without anyone having read the paper.

### But `probe_pdf.py` calls the same file `clean`

The two implementations of the stretch tell disagree completely on this document, and the
newer one is wrong.

`probe_pdf.py` collects candidate lines under `if len(text.strip()) >= 25`, and then runs *both*
the stretch test and the letter-soup test over only those. Destroyed display equations are
**short** — `Ww`, `er`, `ME`, `oe` — so they never enter the sample, and the file is reported
`clean` with 0 stretched lines and 0 garbage passages. `pdf_to_source.flag_stretched()` has no
length floor and sees all seventeen.

The 25-character floor was added for a good reason, stated in its own comment: running heads and
page numbers are legitimately wide per character and were false-positiving. But it is applied
before the garbage test as well as before the stretch test, and garbled mathematics is exactly a
population of short lines. This is the Carroll failure again in a new shape — a document that
reads `clean` while a whole class of its content is letter soup.

It also sits awkwardly with the file's own stated principle. `probe_pdf.py` imports
`detect_columns` from `pdf_to_source` rather than reimplementing it, and says why: "One rule, one
implementation." The stretch rule *is* reimplemented, with a different threshold (`> median *
2.5` against an absolute `11.0 pts/char`) and a different sample.

**Reproducer**

```bash
PYTHONPATH=ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp \
  .venv/bin/python ipsissima-mcp/eval/probe_pdf.py \
    fixtures/ingest/ramsey-1928-mathematical-theory-of-saving.pdf     # clean

.venv/bin/python ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp/pdf_to_source.py \
    fixtures/ingest/ramsey-1928-mathematical-theory-of-saving.pdf /tmp/ramsey.md   # 16 flagged
```

## 6. Robeyns: zero headings from 268 typeset pages, and a splitter that says nothing

pymupdf4llm finds **no headings at all** in a book set in InDesign with six chapters and a full
section hierarchy — the converted file contains not one `#`. That is the case `docs/CORPUS.md`
lists as still wanted ("a `.docx` whose headings are only large body text"), occurring in PDF.

The consequence is downstream. `split_manuscript.py` on the result:

```
== robeyns-2017-wellbeing-oapen.md
   1 top-level sections, 0 part(s), 0 footnote definition(s)
   outside any part: Front matter
```

Exit code 0. The entire 95,550-word book is one section called "Front matter", and nothing warns.
Contrast the Russell, where the headings exist at the wrong level and the splitter does warn,
listing what it found before summarising. **The warning covers "headings at an unexpected level"
and not "no headings at all"**, which is the more complete failure of the two.

`ingest.py` is otherwise fine on this book: 94,210 words, 927 positions, no OCR attempted, 1.9
seconds. It also reports `no back matter found` on a book that ends in a bibliography and an
index, because `BACK_MATTER` is looking for a Markdown heading and there are none.

## 7. Two small ones

**`probe_pdf.py` does not import.** Line 42 puts `../Build scripts` on `sys.path`; the modules
moved to `ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp/` and nothing there is named that. Every invocation
ends in `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'pdf_to_source'`. Every measurement above used
`PYTHONPATH=ipsissima-mcp/src/ipsissima_mcp` as a workaround; the file was not edited.
`MARKITDOWN-FINDINGS.md` line 307 already notes the same stale path in another harness, so this
is a known class with at least one more instance than was recorded.

**`pdf_to_source.py`'s usage line is wrong.** The docstring ends
`python3 pdf_to_source.py <file.pdf> <out.md>   # detect only, report, write nothing`, and
`__main__` calls `convert()` and prints `wrote …`. It writes. The useful sense of the advice —
run it with no Config overrides first and read the report before setting anything — is right, and
was followed throughout; the comment as written is not.

## What this suggests

**Route by document shape, and keep both routes.** The corrected recommendation in
`CONVERTER-FINDINGS.md` was "the PDF's own text layer first, probe, escalate only if damaged".
Nothing here contradicts that, but all four documents probe `clean` and two of them are badly
served by `pdf_to_source.py` anyway. The damage these fixtures expose is not in the text layer's
*characters*; it is in its *geometry* — fragmented lines on the Dewey, two-column paragraph
numbers on the Miller — and the probe does not look at geometry beyond column count.

**The three cheapest fixes, in order of what they buy.** The `references?` singular and the
missing `break` in `article_span()` are each a few characters and together they return 2,358
words of the Dewey. A secondary `x0` sort keyed on clustered `y` extents in `sheet_lines()` is
larger, and it is what takes the Dewey from 1% to something usable. Dropping the 25-character
floor from `probe_pdf.py`'s *garbage* test, while keeping it for the stretch test, is one line
and stops a paper of letter-soup equations reporting `clean`.

**The format gap is unchanged.** The list still has no XML of any kind, no `.odt`, no `.tex`, no
tracked changes, and no scanned PDF without a text layer. Two of those were meant to be closed
today and were closed off by a robots directive and a WAF instead.
